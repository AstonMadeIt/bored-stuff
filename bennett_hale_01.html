The Central Misreading

On Why "Power Asymmetry" Is A Feature, Not A Bug

Chen & Webb argue that the power dynamic is a structural flaw. We argue it's the foundation of consent. Their critique assumes that equality of outcome requires equality of process. This is a category error.

The Core Argument

The women in this system are not seeking "equal power." They are seeking predictable power—a known framework where their autonomy is protected by structure, not threatened by ambiguity. The Principal's "control" is not control over them; it's control over the boundary conditions that make their autonomy possible.

The Lange Power Symmetry Scale

In our 2024 NSF-funded study on consensual non-monogamy (n=2,300), relationships with explicit power structures reported 34% lower anxiety and 41% higher satisfaction than "egalitarian" structures where power was constantly negotiated.

Ambiguity is the enemy of consent. When power is unclear, people test boundaries. When power is explicit, people can choose to engage or disengage with full knowledge.

The "Victim" Fallacy

Chen & Webb's critique infantilizes the women. Dr. Kit March didn't stumble into this—she chose it. She's a chief of cardiothoracic surgery who commands life-and-death power daily. She seeks a space where the structure commands for her.

This is not subjugation. This is consensual submission—a documented psychological need in high-agency individuals.
